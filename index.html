How we testStart a study
What we measure

Efficiency, function, return.

01

Nutrient-use efficiency

Agronomic efficiency, partial factor productivity and apparent recovery of applied N and P.

02

Soil function

Cation exchange capacity, water-holding capacity, aggregate stability, pH buffering.

03

Return per acre

Partial budget analysis: delivered cost against fertilizer displaced and yield retained.

Analysis

A lab result is not a recommendation. We find the rate where response peaks - for a named crop, in a named soil.

Crop, soil texture, blend ratio, placement and weather carried as variables. Confidence stated, including where the data is thin. More rate is not more response, and the point where it stops paying is the number a grower actually needs.

What we test

Anything that goes into the ground and claims to help.

01

Mineral amendments

Pozzolan, zeolite, silicates, gypsum, lime and other rock-derived materials.

02

Fertilizer-efficiency products

Inhibitors, coatings, humic and fulvic products, and anything sold to make a nutrient go further.

03

Biologicals and biostimulants

Microbial inoculants, extracts and metabolites, held to the same evidence standard as everything else.

04

Reclamation materials

Soil-building blends for mine spoil, construction sites and ground taken out of production.

The work. Poor soils are a measurement problem before they are an input problem.

Soils

One material. Six soils. Six answers.

We do not work one region or one soil type. A material that lifts productivity on silt loam can do very little on saline ground, and a client is better served knowing that early.

Method

Four stages. Each one earns the next.

STAGE 01

Characterize

Mineralogy, exchange capacity, nutrient retention in representative soils.

STAGE 02

Greenhouse

Rate trials crossed with full and reduced fertilizer.

STAGE 03

Field

Randomized, replicated plots with cooperating growers.

STAGE 04

Publish

Analysis, economics per acre, open technical report.

We report fertilizer-efficiency and soil-property outcomes only. No pest or disease claims. Figures drawn from existing literature are labeled as such.

Economics

The agronomy has to clear the freight.

An input that works in the plot and loses money at the gate has not worked. We carry delivered cost, application and haul inside the analysis, and report the rate at which value returned overtakes what the grower spent - or report that it does not.

Pipeline

Pozzolan is first through the pipeline. Others follow.

Porous, silica-rich, barely tested in row-crop systems. Cereals, turf and forage grasses first, then pulses, oilseeds and specialty crops, across soils from silt loam to claypan, sand and degraded bottomland. We take on any soil amendment a client needs measured - including the efficacy data most states require before a product can be registered and sold.
