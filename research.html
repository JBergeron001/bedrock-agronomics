Trial design

Rules we do not bend.

DESIGN

Randomized complete block

Four replications minimum. Buffer rows and alleys. Never a single demonstration strip.

TREATMENTS

Control, label rate, 2× label rate

Plus a reduced-fertilizer arm where the claim concerns nutrient efficiency.

ENDPOINTS

Fixed before the season

Measures and the analysis plan are written into the protocol and not revised afterwards.

ANALYSIS

Stated, not selected

Analysis of variance with mean separation, coefficient of variation and significance reported at the level the receiving state accepts.

SCALE

Site-years, not seasons

Most registration reviews want two years at each of several sites, on soils common to that state.

CONDUCT

Written procedures

Standard operating procedures for sampling, handling and data capture. Protocols follow Good Experimental Practice principles.

LABS

Independent

University and accredited commercial laboratories, under chain of custody. No in-house bench.

Replication

Four replications minimum, and all of them get reported.

A strip trial produces a number. A randomized, replicated block produces a number with an interval around it, and the interval is what tells a reviewer whether the difference is real. Where treatments overlap, we say so.

Before the season. Endpoints and the analysis plan are fixed in writing, then measured. Nothing is chosen after the data arrives.

Clients

A product is not a business until someone else has measured it.

Agricultural technology companies reach us with a working product and a credibility gap. Distributors will not carry a line on internal data. State control officials will not register a claim without substantiation. Investors discount a founder’s own trial results in diligence, and growers, who carry the cost of a failed season, discount them further. We produce the evidence that closes that gap, to a standard that holds in front of all four.

CLAIMS

Written first

Each claim is drafted in label wording, then a trial is designed to test that exact wording.

SOILS

As the state defines them

Commonly several soil types common to the state of registration, with at least one site inside it.

RECORD

Reviewer-ready

Objective, materials and methods, conditions, results, discussion, and the qualifications of the people who ran it.

SCOPE

Labels are broader than packaging

Control officials treat brochures and websites as labeling. Claims there need the same substantiation.

We are a research firm, not a registration agent. We produce the data and the report; filing is yours or your counsel’s. The same trial standard carries public work - conservation programs, watershed districts and reclamation projects - where the question is performance on a given soil rather than a label.

Crops

Grasses first. Then the rest.

Grasses take up silicon in amounts comparable to a macronutrient, so a silica-rich amendment has the clearest shot at a measurable response there. The pipeline widens from that starting point.

01

Cereals and grains

Corn, sorghum, wheat, oats, barley, rice.

02

Turf and forage grasses

Golf, sports fields, sod and pasture. Wear tolerance and canopy quality join yield as endpoints.

03

Pulses and legumes

Soybean, dry bean, pea, lentil, alfalfa. Nitrogen is fixed, so the efficiency question becomes phosphorus and potassium.

04

Oilseeds and fiber

Canola, sunflower, flax, cotton.

05

Specialty and horticulture

Vegetables, fruit, nursery and greenhouse, where value per acre carries a higher amendment rate.

06

Reclamation and revegetation

Mine spoil, construction sites, roadsides and other ground being brought back into function.

Variables

One material, many conditions.

SOIL

Sand to heavy clay

Degraded, eroded, compacted, saline, acidic or low in organic matter. The ground that needs help most.

RATE

Rate, blend, placement

Broadcast, banded or incorporated, alone or blended with conventional fertilizer.

SEASON

More than one year

A single season proves little. Weather is a treatment, not an excuse.

Stage two

Controlled growth, before anything reaches a field.

FIELD

Protocol and execution

Claim review, protocol, site and cooperator selection, plot layout, sampling, laboratory coordination, in-season reporting.

DATA

Analysis and modeling

Analysis of variance across site-years, response-curve fitting by crop and soil, partial budget per acre.

OUTPUT

Reporting

Trial report in substantiation format, summary tables, technical bulletins and support for grant applications.

Controlled-environment, phenotyping and analytical work is placed with university and research-institute core facilities. Client work is confidential, data belongs to the client, and publication is by agreement. We say in advance what a study can and cannot support.
